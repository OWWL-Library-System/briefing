## Harold Hacker Fund for the Advancement of Libraries Grant

Each year, Friends & Foundation of the Rochester Public Library offers a grant opportunity through the Harold Hacker Fund for the Advancement of Libraries. This opportunity is open to all RRLC member libraries and systems, all OWWL Library System libraries, and all Monroe County Library System libraries.

Funding supports:

- Education and professional development for librarians and library staff throughout the five-county region served by the Rochester Regional Library Council.
- Innovative library projects and programs at RRLC member libraries, the Monroe County Library, and the OWWL Library System and its member libraries.

Applications are accepted beginning August 31, 2026, and are due by 11:59 PM on September 30, 2026. [Learn more and download the application](https://us.list-manage.com/d9O5Apqivuh?e=c66770b5e0&c2id=89d3980c603b8ae17fa5e8c02c5d7cc1).

## This Week in OSC Audits

### Building Access: Seven Audits, One Pattern

Seven building-access audits this cycle, across six school districts and one regional BOCES, turned up the same problems again and again: duplicate active badges held by the same person, non-employee badges (vendors, volunteers, program partners, substitutes) left active long after they were needed, and shared badges that nobody was tracking. In three of the seven, staff couldn't even physically produce badges that showed as "active" in the system; one district could only locate 24 of the 52 badges auditors requested. One audit also found unverified background checks for non-employees with direct contact with students. Every audit traced back to the same root cause: no written policy assigning anyone responsibility for periodically reviewing who still needs access.

**Takeaways for libraries**

- Check whether your written badge or key policy matches what staff actually do day-to-day, not just what's on paper.
- Set a firm inactivity threshold for non-employee badges (volunteers, contractors, program partners) and confirm someone actually disables them on schedule.
- Put an expiration date on any shared badge or fob issued to a vendor or outside partner, rather than leaving it open-ended.
- Name one staff member responsible for reviewing the full badge list at least once a year for employees and non-employees alike and removing what's no longer needed.
- Keep a physical inventory of every issued key, fob, or badge, and periodically confirm you can actually locate them, not just that a system shows them as "active."
- If volunteers or program partners have unsupervised contact with children, confirm that their background checks were verified directly by your library, not assumed to have been handled elsewhere.

### Cybersecurity: Pay Close Attention to This One

Three audits this cycle, one town and two villages, focused on cybersecurity, and all three found the same foundational gaps: no board-adopted cybersecurity policy, no incident response or contingency plan, no inventory of IT equipment, and no cybersecurity training for staff. The town's audit also found unnecessary accounts still active on its network and no password policy for its board to adopt.

This is the section to read closely. We're still in the middle of our own account-compromise incident, the one flagged in the August 11 briefing, and the pattern in these audits, informal practice standing in for a written policy, is exactly what let that incident go unnoticed for weeks. If your library doesn't have a board-adopted cybersecurity policy, a written incident response plan, or a current IT equipment inventory, treat that as unfinished business, not something to get to eventually.

**Takeaways for libraries**

- Have your board formally adopt a written cybersecurity policy and a basic incident response or contingency plan, rather than relying on informal practice.
- Keep an up-to-date inventory of your IT equipment and require regular cybersecurity awareness training for staff, even if you have only one or two computers.
- Review your active network and cloud accounts against your current staff list on a regular schedule, and disable any accounts left over from former employees or vendors.
- If you rely on an outside IT vendor, put in writing who is responsible for staff training and for maintaining written policies. Don't assume the vendor already covers it.
- If your board hasn't adopted a written password policy, put one in place rather than leaving password strength up to individual habit.

### [A third village: Water and Sewer Billing and Collections](https://www.osc.ny.gov/local-government/audits/village/2026/08/07/village-medina-water-and-sewer-billing-and-collections-2026m-27)

This audit isn't about libraries directly, but the underlying control weaknesses translate well. Auditors found a data-entry error that inflated one customer's usage reading a hundredfold, and a pattern of not monitoring low-consumption accounts for signs of a broken meter (one faulty meter alone cost the village roughly $768 over four years once it was finally replaced). While $296,867 in collections was deposited timely and intact, the clerk-treasurer had no written procedures for billing, collecting, or depositing funds, and delinquent-account rules weren't consistently enforced. Auditors also cited weak segregation of duties and thin supervisory review.

**Takeaways for libraries**

- Put your process for collecting fines, fees, and program payments in writing, spelling out who collects, who records, and who deposits, so no single person handles every step.
- If your board has adopted rules for unpaid balances or fee waivers, check that staff are applying them consistently rather than on a case-by-case basis.

> Across all of these audits, a consistent pattern appears. Problems rarely begin with fraud or misconduct. They begin with small gaps in oversight, unclear documentation, or policies that drift out of alignment with actual practice. Regular review of financial procedures, procurement policies, and board reporting practices helps prevent those small gaps from becoming larger governance failures.
