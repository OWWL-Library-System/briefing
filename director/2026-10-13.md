## Letters to the Governor

**We are asking every library to send a letter to the Governor by Friday, October 30.** The goal is to get as many letters as possible to the Governor's office between now and then. Each letter shows the Governor that libraries matter in every community, and the more letters that arrive, the harder that is to miss.

The letters ask the Governor to increase New York State Library Operating Aid to $188 million in the 2027-28 budget, and to increase funding for the Public Library Construction Aid program to $175 million. Both figures match the New York Library Association's budget requests. Over the last 20 years, Library Operating Aid has increased an average of just 0.8% per year, while inflation over the same period was 67%.

This is a statewide effort. The Public Library System Directors Organization of New York State (PULISDO) is collecting letters from every library system and its member libraries, and will send them to the Governor together with a cover letter from its chair. The OWWL Library System is sending its own letter as well.

### The letter

The letter is below, ready to send as written. You can simply copy and paste it into an email, a document on your library's letterhead, or the Governor's online form, and you are welcome to customize it.

> Dear Governor Hochul:
>
> I am writing on behalf of our library, one of 42 public libraries in the OWWL Library System, to ask that you increase State Library Operating Aid to $188 million and Public Library Construction Aid to $175 million in the 2027-28 budget.
>
> Over the last 20 years, Library Operating Aid has increased an average of 0.8% per year, while inflation rose 67%. Libraries help residents find jobs, navigate government services, and enroll in health coverage. They offer free summer reading, a place for older adults to connect, and refuge in extreme weather. Our buildings are aging as well, and State Aid for Library Construction is needed for roofs, security cameras, electrical upgrades, and new flooring.
>
> Public libraries cannot keep doing more with less. I urge you to commit to increasing State Library Aid. Thank you for your support of our library and the residents we serve.
>
> Sincerely,
>
> [Your name and title]  
> [Your library]

### What to do

1. **Copy the letter above.** It is ready to send as written. You are welcome to customize it.
2. **Put it on your library's letterhead.** Add the date, sign it, and add your name and title.
3. **Send it to the Governor by Friday, October 30** through the [Governor's online contact form](https://www.governor.ny.gov/content/governor-contact-form) (simply copy/paste the letter into the form). 
4. **Email it to** JW Cook, the Governor's regional representative, at JW.Cook@exec.ny.gov. **Copy me on that email** so I know who sent a letter and can track it in the statewide portal.
5. **Mail a paper copy, if you would like.** Address it to The Honorable Kathy Hochul, Governor of New York State, NYS State Capitol Building, Albany, NY 12224.

I will send every letter I receive to the statewide effort.

The System's letter will go out this week and I will copy everyone in on it so you can see it.

## Minimum Standards Compliance Session: Thursday, October 15

RCLS and WLS have contracted with attorney Stephanie Cole Adams for an online education session, and all PULISDO members have been invited to share it with their libraries. It is titled "We Are Ready: Confirming Your Library Complies with the New Minimum Standards for Policies," and it meets Thursday, October 15, from 6:00 to 8:00 PM.

### Why it matters

On January 1, 2027, new minimum standards for internal and external policies take effect for public and association libraries. The standards require these external policies.

- Selection of library materials and reconsideration of that selection.
- Public use of library space and meeting rooms.
- Codes of conduct.
- Confidentiality of library records.

They also require these internal policies.

- Personnel policies that ensure consistent staff management and fair employment practices.
- Disaster preparedness.
- Financial control policies that fulfill the legal and fiduciary responsibilities of the governing body and promote fiscal oversight, accountability, and sustainable management.

### What the session covers

The session focuses on verifying what your library needs, building a work plan if a few things are missing, and confirming compliance before the new year. Template materials to help boards finish on time will be provided.

No registration is required. A recording and the handouts will be available for anyone who cannot attend live. Attendees may self-report 2 CE hours toward the Trustee Education or Public Librarian Certification requirements. Staff, directors, and trustees are all welcome.
