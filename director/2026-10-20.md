## CANS Notice: Advance Notice for Equipment Installs

**Advance notice for phone, printer, and security camera installs**

As part of our ongoing efforts to maintain stable, secure, and efficient network operations across the System, we ask that **libraries provide at least one week of advance notice before installing or replacing security cameras, printers, or phone systems.**

While we are happy to consult, libraries are welcome to select vendors for these devices on their own. However, it is important that OWWL HQ is informed ahead of time so we can prepare for any network or configuration considerations that may arise. This helps us ensure that installations proceed smoothly and that devices integrate properly with existing infrastructure.

**How to notify us**

Please email `support` with:

- The type of device being installed (camera, printer, or phone system)
- The make/model (if known)
- The scheduled installation date(s)
- The vendor/installer (optional but helpful)

Providing this information at least one week before installation allows us to support you effectively. Thank you.

## CANS Notice: Microsoft Office 2013, 2016, 2019, and 2021 End of Support

Microsoft Office 2013, 2016, 2019, and 2021 have all reached End of Support. Microsoft no longer provides security updates, bug fixes, or technical support for these versions.

### What this means for your library

We are not requiring any library to upgrade to Office 2024. Computers that already have an older version installed can continue to use it.

However, when your library purchases new computers, we can no longer install Office 2013, 2016, or 2019 on them. For new computers, you will need to choose one of two options:

- Purchase Microsoft Office 2024, the current supported version.
- Switch to LibreOffice, a free office suite that we can install and configure for you.

A note on Office 2021: Office 2021 reached End of Support on October 13, 2026. We will not deny a request to install it on a new computer. However, because it no longer receives security updates, we encourage libraries to purchase Office 2024 or use LibreOffice instead.

### About LibreOffice

We have heard concerns from some libraries about LibreOffice. Two common concerns can be addressed through configuration:

- **Familiar layout:** LibreOffice can be set up with a tabbed ribbon interface that closely resembles Microsoft Office, so staff and patrons can find tools where they expect them. (We've included screenshots below for comparison.)
- **File compatibility:** LibreOffice can be set to save in Microsoft formats (.docx, .xlsx, .pptx) by default, so files can be shared with Office users without extra steps.
- **Familiar fonts:** LibreOffice's default fonts can be changed to familiar options such as Times New Roman, Arial, or Calibri, so new documents look the way staff and patrons expect.

If you have questions or would like help deciding which option fits your library, please contact `support`

## New Labor Law on Employee Access to Personnel Records Starts November 8

Governor Hochul signed a new law on September 9, 2026 that adds Section 210-b to the Labor Law, titled Access to Personnel Records. It takes effect November 8, 2026. Libraries are covered. The law defines an employer to include a governmental entity or public employer.

**There is no regulation or agency guidance on this law yet, so please stay tuned.** The Governor signed it with the understanding that the Legislature will clean up certain ambiguities in its next session. I will share more as guidance becomes available. What follows is limited to what the statute itself says.

### What the law says

- **Who can ask.** Current and former employees may request their personnel record.
- **What counts as a personnel record.** It is a record the employer keeps that identifies an employee and is used, has been used, or may be used in decisions about qualifications for employment, promotion, transfer, additional compensation, or disciplinary action. Records held for the employer by a contractor are included. Personal information about another person is excluded when sharing it would be a clearly unwarranted invasion of that person's privacy.
- **Copies are free.** On a written request, the employer must provide a copy at no cost within five business days.
- **Review is limited to twice a year.** An employee may review the record no more than twice in a calendar year. A review that follows a notice about negative information does not count toward that limit.
- **Negative information requires notice.** The employer must notify the employee within 10 days of placing information in the record that could affect employment, promotion, transfer, pay, or discipline.
- **Employees can dispute what is in the record.** The employer and employee may agree to correct or remove information. If they do not agree, the employee may submit a written statement. It becomes part of the record and goes with the information when it is shared with a third party.
- **The complete record must be kept.** The employer must retain it without deletions or expungement of information from the date of employment until three years after employment ends.
- **Violations carry a fine of $500 to $2,500.** The Attorney General enforces the law. The law also prohibits retaliation against an employee who exercises these rights.

### What the law does not say

The statute does not say whether the 10-day notice period counts calendar days or business days. Only the five-day copy deadline is written in business days. The Governor's approval also noted open questions, including the scope of the term "personnel record" and whether an employer must create records it does not already keep. The follow-up legislation is expected to address these, and I will share what I learn.
