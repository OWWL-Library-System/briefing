## 2027 OverDrive Funding Commitment

Kelly has sent the 2027 OverDrive Funding Commitment Agreement and a funding summary that shows the amount requested from each member library. Member library contributions are held in a pooled fund account with OverDrive and used to buy downloadable fiction.

### What to do

Complete the Funding Commitment Form and email it to Kelly by **Tuesday, November 10**. The form asks you to confirm your commitment amount and to choose when OWWL should invoice your library.

- [2027 OverDrive Funding Commitment Agreement and form](https://owwl-library-system.github.io/briefing/assets/images/uploads/OverDrive%20Funding%20Commitment%20Agreement%202027.pdf)
- [2027 OverDrive Funding Commitment Summary](https://owwl-library-system.github.io/briefing/assets/images/uploads/2027%20OverDrive%20Funding%20Commitment%20Summary.pdf)

### Details

- **Amount.** The 2027 request is a 3% increase on your 2026 commitment. To help you budget, plan on a 3% increase over your prior commitment each year.
- **Invoicing.** OWWL invoices once or twice a year, based on your choice, and forwards what it collects to OverDrive. The invoicing dates are January 2027 and July 2027. If you check both, your commitment is divided equally. You may also pay the full amount at either date.
- **FFRPL Grant.** If your library receives funds from the FFRPL Grant, do not include that amount in your 2027 commitment. There will be a separate form for you to complete.
- **OWWL's share.** In 2026, OWWL contributed $15,000 from its own budget to the OverDrive collection.

Please contact Kelly or me with any questions. Thank you for your continued support of our OverDrive Fiction Collection.

## Minimum Standards Policies Due January 1, 2027

This is a reminder that **January 1, 2027** is the deadline for your board to approve the policies that Minimum Standards now requires. A library that is missing any of them will be out of compliance with Minimum Standards.

### The required policies

- [Selection of Library Materials and Reconsideration](https://docs.owwl.org/pub/Community/PolicyLibrary/Selection%20of%20Library%20Materials%20and%20Reconsideration.docx)
- [Public Usage of Library Space and Meeting Rooms](https://docs.owwl.org/pub/Community/PolicyLibrary/Public%20Usage%20of%20Library%20Space%20and%20Meeting%20Rooms%20Policy.docx)
- [Code of Conduct](https://docs.owwl.org/pub/Community/PolicyLibrary/Code%20of%20Conduct%20Policy.docx)
- [Confidentiality of Library Records](https://docs.owwl.org/pub/Community/PolicyLibrary/Confidentiality%20of%20Library%20Records%20Policy.docx)
- [Personnel Policies](https://docs.owwl.org/Community/EmployeeHandbookTemplateProject) (the Employee Handbook from HR Works)
- [Disaster Preparedness](https://docs.owwl.org/pub/Community/PolicyLibrary/Disaster%20Plan%20Template.docx)
- Financial Control Policies (see the Financial Controls section of the [Policy Library](https://docs.owwl.org/Community/PolicyLibrary))

### How a policy takes effect

Policies that apply to the public, such as the code of conduct and the meeting room policy, must be posted on the library's website. Internal policies, such as the employee handbook, do not need to be posted. Every policy should be reviewed at least every five years and formally adopted by the board at an open meeting with a recorded vote, and the motion and the vote belong in the minutes. The State Library's page on [Minimum Standards policies](https://nyslibrary.libguides.com/publiclibrarystandards/policies) has helpful information on what each policy should cover.

### Drafts are available

If your library needs a draft, the [Policy Library](https://docs.owwl.org/Community/PolicyLibrary) on OWWL Docs has templates for the required policies and many of the recommended ones. Please keep in mind that these policies already build in the legal requirements that apply to them. I would encourage you not to write a policy from scratch unless someone has done the research to understand the laws that affect it. A template is a starting point, so replace the placeholders, remove the policy notes, and confirm that it reflects how your library actually operates before it goes to your board.

## Congratulations to Red Jacket Community Library

Red Jacket Community Library was named one of five 2026-2027 recipients of the Harold Hacker Fund for the Advancement of Libraries. The Friends & Foundation of the Rochester Public Library manages the fund, and the Rochester Regional Library Council facilitates the application process.

Red Jacket's grant will expand its VOX Books collection by about 15 read-along titles. VOX Books are hardcover picture and early-reader books with a permanently attached audio reader. ## This Week in OSC Audits

Below is a summary of recent Office of the State Comptroller audits. These audits examine school districts, villages, and a county agency across New York. The settings vary, but the themes are familiar ones for any public institution, including libraries.

### [School Districts and BOCES: Building Access](https://www.osc.ny.gov/local-government/audits/school-district/2026/10/09/building-access-schools-2026-ms-2)

Auditors reviewed how 20 school districts and BOCES managed and monitored building access accounts and devices. Every one of them (100%) had active accounts and devices that were no longer needed. Nineteen did not properly monitor shared devices, 18 had employees with duplicate active devices, and 12 had no written policy on who manages and monitors accounts. Half had non-employees with duplicate active devices.

**Takeaways for libraries**

- Keep a current list of who holds a key, key card, or code, and remove access when someone leaves or changes roles.
- Assign one person to manage and review building access, and put that responsibility in writing.
- Treat shared and duplicate devices as a risk to monitor, not a convenience to ignore.

### [A School District: Information Technology Asset Management](https://www.osc.ny.gov/local-government/audits/school-district/2026/10/09/palmyra-macedon-central-school-district-information-technology-asset)

Auditors found that a school district's board had not adopted an IT asset inventory policy. Inventory records did not include all IT assets, and none had adequate tracking information. Annual physical counts were not documented, and officials could not locate one of 50 sampled assets, a printer costing about $400. The district's IT purchases totaled about $655,100 over the audit period.

**Takeaways for libraries**

- A short inventory policy that sets what gets tracked, who tracks it, and how often it is counted gives the board something to measure against.
- Record location, assigned user, and purchase date for each device, and document the annual count.
- Even a small missing item is worth following up, because it shows whether the tracking works.

### [A Village: Treasurer's Records and Reports](https://www.osc.ny.gov/local-government/audits/village/2026/10/09/village-moravia-treasurers-records-and-reports-2026m-91)

Auditors found that a village treasurer did not record or report transfers out totaling more than $16.8 million, and did not record receipts and transfers in totaling more than $11.8 million. Bank reconciliations were not always accurate. The 2024-25 annual financial report was filed 115 days late, and the reports for 2023-24 and 2024-25 did not agree with the village's records. Officials agreed with the six recommendations.

**Takeaways for libraries**

- Reconcile bank statements to the books every month, and have someone other than the person who keeps the books review the reconciliation.
- Compare each annual report to the underlying records before it is filed.
- Treat a late filing as a signal to look for a larger problem.

### [A Village: Payroll and Employee Benefits](https://www.osc.ny.gov/local-government/audits/village/2026/10/09/village-walden-payroll-and-employee-benefits-2025m-105)

Auditors identified at least $144,767 in payments that were unsupported, unauthorized, inaccurate, or poorly documented. The largest piece, $87,265, was for time worked without support. The rest included $28,218 in separation errors, $17,854 in unsupported overtime, $7,857 in unauthorized leave accruals, and $3,573 in payroll errors. Auditors also found 617 unauthorized shift changes, 289 leave charges without approval, and 24 leave charges that exceeded accrued balances.

**Takeaways for libraries**

- Require approved timesheets before payroll is processed, and approve overtime and leave in advance.
- Check separation payouts against the policy or contract before they are issued.
- Make sure leave accruals follow what the board has authorized.

### [A County Industrial Development Agency: Website Transparency](https://www.osc.ny.gov/local-government/audits/county/2026/10/16/wayne-county-industrial-development-agency-website-transparency-2026m-85)

Auditors found that an industrial development agency generally maintained a transparent website, with minor issues reviewed with officials. The single recommendation was to improve website transparency processes so the public can easily navigate the site.

**Takeaways for libraries**

- Check that your meeting notices, minutes, budget, policies, and annual report are posted and easy to find.
- Review the site on a schedule so postings do not fall out of date.

### [A School District: Audit Follow-Up on Lead Testing](https://www.osc.ny.gov/local-government/audits/school-district/2026/10/16/ravena-coeymans-selkirk-central-school-district-audit-follow-s9-25-7-f)

This follow-up reviewed progress on a 2025 audit of lead testing and reporting. Of seven original recommendations, three were fully implemented and four, on sampling, testing, and remediation, were only partially implemented. In one testing cycle, 61 of 322 water outlets (19%) were not sampled or properly exempted. Of 288 outlets sampled, 22 exceeded the lead action level, and 10 of those 22 remained in service without follow-up testing or effective controls.

**Takeaways for libraries**

- When an audit, inspection, or insurance review makes a recommendation, assign an owner and a date, and report progress to the board.
- Health and safety follow-up deserves the same attention as financial follow-up.

> Across all of these audits, a consistent pattern appears. Problems rarely begin with fraud or misconduct. They begin with small gaps in oversight, unclear documentation, or policies that drift out of alignment with actual practice. Regular review of financial procedures, procurement policies, and board reporting practices helps prevent those small gaps from becoming larger governance failures.
