## Evergreen Closed Dates Reminder from Dan

If you haven't already, please make sure to add your 2027 closed dates in Evergreen in the next month or so. Having them in by the end of November will ensure that future checkouts aren't impacted. Adding closed dates affects future checkouts' due dates and overdue fine accruals.

Your closed dates are mostly holidays when your library won't be open. If you are typically open on Saturdays year-round except for during the summer, you will also have to add closed dates for your summer Saturdays.

**[Please review this OWWL Docs page before adding any closed dates](https://docs.owwl.org/Evergreen/EvergreenClosedDatesEditor)**. Please email `evergreen` if you have any questions or if you would like a walkthrough on how to add your 2027 closed dates.

This is also an excellent opportunity to **[review your library's hours](https://owwl.org/libraries/)** to confirm their accuracy; Evergreen also relies on these. If your hours aren't correct, please let us know by emailing `support`.

## Free Adult Learning with the Pfaudler Lecture Series

Yesterday, I met with Chris Ryan, former Avon Board President and former OWWL Library System Board President. Chris is involved with the Osher Lifelong Learning Institute (OLLI) at RIT and thought libraries might want to be part of its [Pfaudler Lecture Series](https://www.rit.edu/osher/lecture-series). Chris suggested that libraries set up the Zoom lecture on-site as structured, free adult programming. The Fall season is already well underway, but she said she is happy to send flyers to interested libraries for the Winter series (more on that below).

The Pfaudler Lecture Series is a free program for adults, held in the Fall, Winter, and Spring. The lectures take place on Thursdays from 12:15 to 1:30 PM and are offered over Zoom. A library could set up a computer, log into the Zoom group, and host a group of patrons who watch the lecture together.

This is easy passive adult programming. The lectures are already planned and presented, so the library only needs to provide the space, the screen, and a welcome. It is also a good fit for adult education, and it is free for patrons and for the library.

Chris said OLLI can get flyers out to libraries that are interested in "hosting" these events once the partnership is set up. To give you an idea of what the series looks like, you can see the current lectures on the [OLLI lecture series page](https://www.rit.edu/osher/lecture-series).

**If your library would like to host the Pfaudler Lecture Series, email Megan McLaughlin by November 10,** and we'll get some flyers for the upcoming sessions for you.
